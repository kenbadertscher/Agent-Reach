Why I'd hold off:

	1.	LinkedIn and Reddit only work through your logins. For Reddit, the guide itself says there's no way in without being logged in. Its preferred route is OpenCLI: an npm package plus a Chrome extension that drives your real Chrome, using whatever accounts you're logged into. For us that means GitHub, GMail, and more. One misled agent, or one bad update to that extension, could then act on any of those accounts.
	2.	The agent's instructions can change after you install it. The skill tells the agent to fetch and follow guides from raw.githubusercontent.com/Panniantong/agent-reach/main/..., and to update by installing the latest main branch, which isn't pinned to any version. Whatever the author, or anyone who takes over their account, pushes later becomes instructions your agent follows. Forking doesn't help, because the skill still points at their repo.
	3.	It installs third-party tools I didn't review: @jackwener/opencli, mcporter, twitter-cli, bilibili-cli, boss-agent-cli, and for LinkedIn, uvx mcp-server-linkedin@latest. That last one always fetches the newest version and stores a LinkedIn login. Agent-Reach is mostly a router for these tools, so this is where the real risk sits.
	4.	Your research goes to outside services. Searches go to Exa, and page reads that fall back to r.jina.ai send every URL you read to Jina. That's fine for public research, but not for anything involving clients.
	5.	It breaks LinkedIn's and Reddit's terms. Logged-in scraping is exactly what their terms forbid. The tool's own guide recommends a secondary account, because the account can get banned.
	6.	Much of it doesn't fit your needs. About half the channels are Chinese platforms (Xiaohongshu, Bilibili, Boss Zhipin, Xueqiu, Xiaoyuzhou).
